Overview

For most applicants, the route into Lithuania starts with a national (type D) visa or a work-based residence permit tied to a confirmed employment offer, typically beginning with a Schengen visa and converting, once in-country, into a 2-year renewable residence permit. Lithuania’s appeal is a materially lower cost of living and entry compared with Western Europe, while still offering the long-term mobility benefits of EU residence once your status is established.

Is there a digital nomad visa for Lithuania?+

How long is the initial residence permit valid?+

Typically 2 years, and renewable subject to continued eligibility.
